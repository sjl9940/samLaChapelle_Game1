A small browser game by Sam LaChapelle

Conversations
on the Street

Move through three changing New York boroughs while managing the pressure of being approached. Busy sidewalks, traffic, visible construction, storefronts, and changing street moods make each route feel different.

Play the three levels ↗

Domain knowledge

Attention has a cost

The project draws on the designer’s experience avoiding people who ask for money on New York streets. The novice assumption in the brief is that being nice should be enough. The intended shift is to notice attention, distance, time, and money, then judge when to be direct and when limited resources change the choice.

How it plays

Observe → judge → act

Weave through visible street obstacles while pedestrians and cars move around you. A yellow question mark identifies scammers; unmarked pedestrians are passersby. Brooklyn scammers react only when the player faces them on the same screen half, so distance and a wide detour can avoid an encounter; Manhattan uses range and line of sight; every visible Queens scammer can spot the player, with progressively more pursuers: two in Brooklyn, three in Manhattan, and five in Queens. Manhattan and Queens give a two-second no-contact pause after a correct response; the crowd remains. Scammers call out and close in. The road stays linear, and the game page uses distinct colors for each borough. Read scammer behavior and choose Polite, Firm, Pay, or Walk away. Brooklyn clues align with the right response; Manhattan sometimes gives mixed signals; Queens randomizes the right choice across all four, even for familiar dialogue. The smaller Brooklyn and Manhattan crowds remain after correct answers. Their pixel sprites shift by borough, and every level has a distinct funk track that grows more stressful. Wrong dialogue choices flash red and may force a payment after three mistakes. Low money brings a pulsing red warning and siren. Reach an exit for a cash bonus, then continue to the next borough.

Three-level progression

Survival / Management / Nightmare

Brooklyn / Survival introduces warm storefronts and street-reading. Manhattan / Management adds a denser, faster rush. Queens / Nightmare brings an unsettled night mood and citywide scammer detection, with a limited group pursuing at once.

Weather and chaotic mood are environmental details; they do not affect gameplay.

Designer statement

Know when attention changes the situation.

“As a New Yorker I know the extent people will go to take your money.” The game turns a familiar street judgment into a system of movement, attention, time, money, and uncertain dialogue outcomes.
