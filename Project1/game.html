Each borough has distinct building and scammer sprites, crowds, street details, and original funk music that grows more stressful. A yellow ? marks a scammer; unmarked passersby are ambient and do not start conversations. In Brooklyn, a scammer reacts only when you face them and you are on the same half of the screen; staying far away or on the opposite half lets you pass. Manhattan uses range and line of sight with 18 scammers and a two-second no-contact pause after a correct response; in Queens every visible scammer spots you. Brooklyn clues align with the right response, and the smaller scammer crowd stays in the street after a correct answer. Manhattan has 20% mixed-signal encounters where the clue may not match the right response. In Queens, the dialogue is shuffled and the right move is randomized across Polite, Firm, Pay, and Walk away, so a familiar line can have a different answer. The crowd stays after correct answers in Brooklyn and Manhattan; the answered Queens scammer backs off briefly. Pursuit pressure grows by level: up to two scammers can chase in Brooklyn, three in Manhattan, and five in Queens. Scammers spread apart to limit clumping. Manhattan and Queens give a two-second no-contact pause after a correct response; the crowd remains in the street. Some encounters escalate through three questions and force payment after three wrong answers; this is more likely in Queens. A wrong answer flashes the screen red, and a hit by traffic brings a horn. Exit rewards carry into the next borough.
