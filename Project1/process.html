Human–AI development timeline

How the game took shape.

This timeline records the current game and design decisions. It will keep changing with the project.

01

Brief review · 13 Oct 2026

Indexed the source material

Read the 16-section brief and viewed the hand-drawn system graph. Noted ambiguities in challenge presets, conversation outcomes, and environment data before implementation.

02

Student design decisions · 13 Oct 2026

Defined three escalating levels

The student named Survival, Management, and Nightmare; clarified that later levels require resource judgment and Nightmare increases enemy speed, spawn frequency, and detection range. Weather and chaotic mood are environmental only. Buildings shape the street path.

03

First playable milestone · 13 Oct 2026

Built the first exhibition and game

Created the home page, three sequential levels, randomized streets, movement, dialogue, payment, time and money feedback, and a route guarantee.

04

Movement and city-life update · 13 Oct 2026

Expanded the street into a scrolling city

Replaced tile-by-tile movement with continuous walking and a camera that follows along a long route. Added moving pixel people, later-level pursuit, limited visibility, city ambience, and a caught warning sound.

05

Street navigation update · 13 Oct 2026

Made the player weave through the avenue

Added alternating building barriers that require upward and downward movement, moved the four response actions over the game view, brightened the street and characters, added mandatory car-collision costs, and made nearby scammers scatter during a conversation. Replaced the constant audio bed with a browser-generated funk groove over lighter traffic ambience.

The student’s later browser playtest exposed issues with one-direction completion, obstacle visibility, car impact, and scammer tension.

06

Playtest-driven redesign · 13 Oct 2026

Turn the street pressure into readable choices

After the student reported one-direction play, invisible barriers, ineffective-feeling cars, and weak scammer tension, the game gained three borough moods, visible construction and sidewalk details, moving pedestrians, 24 clue-bearing scammer lines, callouts and warning indicators, and a low-wallet red flash with a repeating alarm. A wrong response now shows an X and disappears; level-specific funk changes with the borough. Four encounter actions remain.

The student played all three levels and reported that the warning sound increased stress, while the scammers’ skin tone reinforced a negative stereotype and the challenge still needed stronger consequences.

07

Playtest response · 13 Oct 2026

Revised character representation and stakes

Changed every scammer sprite to a light-skin palette while keeping their clothing and appearance varied. Increased detection ranges; added three-question forced-payment encounters, more frequent in Queens; added a one-second red flash for wrong answers and a horn on car impact. Boroughs now use visibly different building and scammer sprite treatments and distinct generated music arrangements that grow faster and more tense. Exits award a carried cash bonus, celebrate with cheers, and show clear YOU WIN / YOU LOSE screens with reset or next-level controls.

The student then played all three levels and reported that anxiety improved but character clarity, Queens intensity, and car reactions still needed work.

08

Clarity and Queens swarm update · 13 Oct 2026

Made character roles readable and level 3 overwhelming

Changed all human sprite skin palettes to light tones in response to the student’s concern about racial stereotyping, removed the ambiguous carried-looking accessory, and added a yellow question marker to scammers while leaving passersby unmarked. Queens now makes every visible scammer spot and swarm the player from across the screen; the swarm waits during dialogue and resumes afterward. Cars stop briefly on impact, honk, and then resume moving right. The game page explains why some people approach and others do not.

The latest edits have not yet been playtested.

09

Playtest response · 13 Oct 2026

Kept the Queens threat while reopening escape space

Queens scammers remain screen-wide aware, but only three pursue simultaneously and they steer apart instead of collapsing into a crowd. Scammer starting positions are spaced out. Building collision now treats the rear roof rows as walkable and keeps the street-facing facade solid. The route bends are now visible and vary by borough. The game page now has a light-blue base with stronger Brooklyn, Manhattan, and Queens color themes.

The three-chaser limit, facade depth, bend visibility, and color themes need a fresh playtest.

10

Playtest correction · 13 Oct 2026

Reopened the Queens exit and simplified the street

After the student reported the curved route and building behavior felt like a downgrade, restored the linear road. A correct response to one Queens scammer now makes the whole group leave. Buildings have a two-row street-facing under-awning strip the player can enter; the player sprite disappears while underneath, and the rest of the building remains solid.

The Queens crowd departure and under-building passage need a fresh playtest.

11

Playtest correction · 13 Oct 2026

Kept the Queens crowd after a correct read

The student said clearing every scammer made the level too easy and asked for a two-second no-contact window after a correct answer instead. Now the answered scammer backs off, the remaining scammers stay in the street, and no scammer can chase, catch, or initiate a conversation during the brief pause. Queens now has a denser population and allows up to five separated pursuers.

The brief grace period and renewed crowd pressure need a fresh playtest.

12

Difficulty tuning · 13 Oct 2026

Raised pressure in Brooklyn and Manhattan

The student said Brooklyn and Manhattan felt too easy and confirmed Queens is ready. Increased street population, scammer speed and detection in the first two boroughs, and the number of simultaneous pursuers now rises from two in Brooklyn to three in Manhattan and five in Queens. The two-second no-contact pause remains Queens-only.

The first two levels’ new pressure and Queens pause timing need a fresh playtest.

13

Dialogue and avoidance update · 13 Oct 2026

Made more than a firm response viable

Added twelve scammer openings that cue when walking away is the right move and eight polite openings, bringing the pool to 44. Adjusted outcome weighting so polite responses, firm boundaries, and walking away are all similarly likely to be the correct read. Brooklyn now lets the player avoid a scammer by staying far away, facing elsewhere, or remaining on the opposite half of the screen; a scammer reacts only when the player faces them on the same half. Updated the pages to explain all 44 dialogue lines and the avoidance rule.

The new dialogue balance and Brooklyn screen-half rule need a playtest.

14

Encounter and uncertainty update · 13 Oct 2026

Kept the street crowded and made later reads less certain

Reduced Brooklyn and Manhattan scammer counts to 12 and 18. Starting a conversation no longer sends nearby scammers away, and a correctly answered Brooklyn or Manhattan scammer stays in the street after briefly backing off. Brooklyn keeps clue-aligned answers. In 20% of Manhattan encounters, the right response differs from the behavior clue. Queens independently randomizes the target among Polite, Firm, Pay, and Walk away, regardless of the opening line, making familiar dialogue potentially misleading.

The Manhattan uncertainty rate, Queens fairness, and remaining-crowd pressure need a fresh playtest.

15

Manhattan crowd adjustment · 13 Oct 2026

Reduced the Manhattan scammer group

After the student reported that Manhattan scammers clumped together, reduced that level’s scammer count from 18 to 10. The level description and README now reflect the smaller group.

The reduced crowd still needs a playtest to confirm it feels less clumped.

16

Manhattan pause update · 13 Oct 2026

Restored the larger crowd and added a brief reset window

Restored Manhattan from 10 to 18 scammers after the student said the smaller group did not address clumping. After a correct answer, all Manhattan chasers pause for two seconds while the answered scammer remains in the street. Updated the current level descriptions to match.

The restored crowd and pause need a playtest to check whether they reduce clumping.
